# Ship a real alarm first, add strangers later

Build Wake Mate in this order: make the alarm trustworthy, narrow matching to one dense niche with a ping-only interaction, then add push and realtime, and only afterwards voice notes or text. The alarm is the product. Expo's `delivery: 'alarmClock'` option (which uses `setAlarmClock`) plus `USE_EXACT_ALARM` fixes the biggest timing defect in about 1-2 days, but it still produces only a notification, not a looping ringer with a lock-screen takeover. A small in-repo Kotlin Expo module for that ringer is a 1-2 week job, and third-party libraries are not a safe shortcut because Notifee was archived in April 2026. On the social side, the design that keeps Play compliance cheap is 18+ only, generated nicknames, preset "I'm up" pings, in-app block and report, a published child-safety page, and an account-deletion path. The cold-start fix is to quantise wake times to 15-minute slots in one region and one niche, which the draft `backend_design_patch_v2.sql` already starts. Push (Expo Push over FCM) and realtime (Supabase Realtime) cost $0 to roughly $60/month through 10k users. Live voice is optional and cheapest on LiveKit, Agora or Daily, and the $399/month Stream Chat floor is not justified for a pilot. The main unknowns are real-device OEM behaviour, how Play reviewers treat the exact-alarm and full-screen-intent declarations, and whether a ping-only app counts as "random chat".

## Alarm reliability decides whether the app is credible

Android offers a clear ladder of alarm APIs. Inexact alarms can fire up to an hour late on Android 12+, and the exact methods rank `setExact` below `setExactAndAllowWhileIdle`, which in turn ranks below `setAlarmClock`, whose delivery time "the system never adjusts" and which leaves low-power modes if needed ([Android alarms guide](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/alarms)). Expo's `NotificationDelivery` maps onto this: `'bestEffort'` (the default) uses `setExactAndAllowWhileIdle`, and OEMs may defer it by minutes, while `'alarmClock'` uses `setAlarmClock` and shows a status-bar alarm icon ([Expo Notifications reference](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/notifications/)). Without an exact-alarm permission, `alarmClock` silently falls back to `bestEffort`. The earlier code audit found that `src/lib/alarm.ts` uses a daily trigger with the default delivery, requests only the notification permission, and plays the default notification sound on a normal channel. Today's alarm can therefore slip by minutes with no warning to the user. The first change is one line plus a permission check: set `delivery: 'alarmClock'`, call `getExactAlarmPermissionsAsync`, and show a visible "alarm may be late" state if the check fails. One detail needs checking against the installed typings: the research notes disagree on whether `delivery` sits on the Daily trigger or only on other trigger types.

Permissions and Play policy point the same way. All exact methods throw `SecurityException` without `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` or `USE_EXACT_ALARM`. Since Android 14, `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` is denied by default for new installs targeting 13+, and alarm-clock apps are told to declare `USE_EXACT_ALARM`, which is granted at install and cannot be revoked ([Android 14 changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/schedule-exact-alarms)). Google Play allows `USE_EXACT_ALARM` only when precisely timed actions are core, user-facing functionality, such as alarm and timer apps, and requires a Play Console declaration ([Play permissions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888170)). Wake Mate qualifies on its face, because the alarm is the headline feature. I recommend declaring `USE_EXACT_ALARM` as primary and dropping `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` unless review pushes back. The notes did not verify whether Play objects to declaring both, which is what `app.json` does today. Full-screen intent follows the same logic: `USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT` is auto-granted only to apps whose core function is alarms or calls, and targeting-14+ apps should submit the declaration ([Play permissions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888170)). If it is denied, the alert falls back to a 60-second heads-up notification ([AOSP FSI limits](https://source.android.com/docs/core/permissions/fsi-limits)). The system also clears alarms on reboot, so the app must reschedule on `BOOT_COMPLETED`. Repeating alarms are all inexact on API 19+, so a "daily exact" alarm must be re-armed as a one-shot each time it fires ([Android alarms guide](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/alarms)). A daily trigger that Expo implements this way may therefore drift, and that is unverified.

Expo's notification stack gets Wake Mate most of the way, and no third-party shortcut exists. The documented API exposes `AndroidAudioUsage.ALARM`, `bypassDnd` and channel `audioAttributes`, but the notes found no documented full-screen intent, looping sound (`INSISTENT`) or reboot guarantee ([Expo Notifications reference](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/notifications/)). Notifee was archived on 7 April 2026, and its maintainers point to expo-notifications ([GitHub](https://github.com/invertase/notifee)). `react-native-alarm-module` was archived in 2023 and does not restore alarms after reboot ([GitHub](https://github.com/jd1378/react-native-alarm-module)). `react-native-alarm-scheduler` v0.1.7 uses `setAlarmClock` and has a config plugin, but its maintenance and SDK 58 compatibility are unconfirmed ([pkgstats](https://www.pkgstats.com/pkg:react-native-alarm-scheduler)). The sound design is my judgement, not a sourced fact: a long bundled tone on an `ALARM`-usage channel approximates a ring for Phase 1, and a looping ringer needs native code. The Phase 2 design is a Kotlin Expo module with its own `setAlarmClock` PendingIntent, a broadcast receiver, a foreground service looping audio on `USAGE_ALARM`, a full-screen alarm activity with snooze and dismiss, and receivers for boot, time change, timezone change, package replacement and exact-alarm permission changes. The platform allows this: exact alarms are exempt from foreground-service background-start limits ([FGS restrictions](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start)). The specific `mediaPlayback` service type behaviour on Android 14-16 was not verified.

OEM battery management is the residual risk, and no code removes it. dontkillmyapp.com rates Huawei, Xiaomi, OnePlus and Samsung 5 out of 5 for aggressive background killing ([dontkillmyapp.com](https://dontkillmyapp.com)). On Samsung, apps unused for about three days move to "sleeping" lists and can miss alarms, and the site states "no known solution on dev end" ([dontkillmyapp.com/samsung](https://dontkillmyapp.com/samsung)). Xiaomi has no documented APIs, and the user must enable Autostart and unrestricted battery ([dontkillmyapp.com/xiaomi](https://dontkillmyapp.com/xiaomi)). Competitor Alarmy tells Pixel users to exclude the app from battery optimisation and avoid memory-clean tools ([Alarmy help](https://alarmy-android.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4596334836505--Google-Pixel)). The plan is a first-run reliability checklist covering notifications, alarms and reminders, full-screen intent, unrestricted battery, a manufacturer-specific autostart pointer, and a one-minute screen-off test alarm. Telemetry comparing alarms scheduled with alarms fired will show OEM kills in the field. The promotion rule is simple: ship Phase 1 to internal testing, and build the native ringer if the test alarm fails on any target OEM or a single tone proves too weak. I would go further and plan on Phase 2 regardless, because a notification that opens Home on tap is not an alarm that wakes a deep sleeper. The earlier audit also noted this, and Phase 2 is also what makes the full-screen-intent declaration honest.

## Cold start is solved by narrowing, not by growing

I found no public "Alarm Mate" listing for stranger wake-up matching; the nearest hits were an iOS calendar alarm app and "Alarm Friend", which pairs manually added friends ([Alarm Friend listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6756028457)). The real precedents are Wakie, which called you at your wake time and placed an automated call when nobody was online ([ABC7](https://abc7ny.com/app-technology-android-iphone/433729/)), and Focusmate, which matches partners on a fixed time grid shortly before the session ([Focusmate FAQ](https://www.focusmate.com/faq)). Early Wakie coverage flagged harassment risk from voice calls with strangers ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/224827/wakie-for-ios-wants-you-to-ditch-the-alarm-clock-and-wake-up-with-strangers.html)). Andrew Chen's "atomic network" argument is that a product should start with the smallest network dense enough to retain everyone ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/atomic-network)). For Wake Mate, the inference is one region by one wake slot on a 15-minute grid by one niche, such as early risers or shift workers. This fits the earlier finding that exact-UTC-minute pairing makes a match nearly impossible. Quantising to 15-minute slots was the fix tested in the earlier patch, where 07:14 and 07:01 paired. Fallbacks should run in order: widen the window by 15 then 30 minutes, form a small trio "wake room" rather than leave an odd user alone, and only then use a labelled non-human fallback. Hiding that a fallback is a bot invites trust and Play deception concerns, which the notes flag as unverified.

No-show handling and retention rest on thinner evidence. Focusmate applies a 5-minute grace period and an attendance score ([Focusmate blog](https://www.focusmate.com/blog/feature-update-july-2018-attendance-score-booking-limits/)). Duolingo's product lead says retention jumps around a 7-day streak, but the evidence is company-reported with no independent study ([Lenny's Podcast summary](https://podcasts-distilled-hugo-content.pages.dev/lennys-podcast-product-career-growth/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks-jackson-shuttleworth-group-pm-retention-team/)). So streaks are a hypothesis to A/B test, not a given. The design that follows is a personal streak with a shared "mornings together" layer, so one person's no-show never breaks another's streak. A no-show lowers the absentee's match priority rather than banning them, and the person who showed gets an instant re-match for the next slot. The earlier patch already covers the server mechanics: single standing slot per user, partner promotion on leave, ejection on report, and an `mark_awake` window of slot minus 10 minutes to slot plus 3 hours. The unfixed gap is the standing slot itself, since a match today covers one date, so the app must re-join daily or a server job must do it.

## Safety and Play compliance are cheap if the interaction stays minimal

Three Play policies apply, and the interaction design controls how heavy each one is. The UGC policy requires terms acceptance, a definition of objectionable content, ongoing moderation, and in-app blocking for one-to-one interaction features; reporting is required for public UGC and closed or verified groups ([Play UGC policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876937)). The Child Safety Standards policy covers anonymous chat apps, random chat apps whose core function is connecting strangers, and any app declaring Social or Dating, regardless of whether children use it. It requires a public CSAE-standards web page naming the app and developer, an in-app way to raise concerns, a named CSAM point of contact in Play Console, and compliance with child-safety law ([Play Child Safety Standards](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14747720)). The account-deletion policy requires an in-app deletion path and a web link if users can create accounts in-app, anonymous accounts included, and the Data safety answers must match ([Play account deletion](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)). The page I read gave no Child Safety deadline, so the August 2026 dates in the earlier report rest on third-party articles and should be treated as unconfirmed. Because the cost of compliance is low, the sensible stance is to comply now, which means shipping block, report, a feedback contact, the safety page and the deletion link in the first public release even though the first interaction is only a ping.

The interaction ladder is the main safety lever. Rung one is a ping-only "I'm up" plus preset messages, generated nicknames and no free text, voice or images, which shrinks UGC to nearly nothing. Rung two adds filtered, rate-limited free text, and rung three is live voice, which I recommend avoiding given the Wakie precedent. Even a nickname chosen by the user is UGC, so use generated names. Anti-abuse should combine Play Integrity with other signals; Google says its account-activity signal separates likely genuine users from automation but must not be the only defence, and its terms forbid using it to fingerprint users ([Play Integrity account activity](https://developer.android.com/google/play/integrity/account-activity)). Supabase's anonymous sign-ins recommend CAPTCHA and cap sign-ups per IP at 30 per hour ([Supabase anonymous sign-ins](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-anonymous)), and the earlier audit found neither wired in. Pair these with per-account rate limits (the patch's limiter), new-account probation, auto-unmatch after repeated reports, and server-side enforcement of every rule. Per-user limits do not stop mass anonymous account creation, so the CAPTCHA and Integrity checks matter.

Minors are the hardest policy question because early-morning school-day use makes teen uptake likely. My recommendation is 18+ only, with a neutral age screen at first run, no pre-filled birth year, and no retry-by-editing. The amended COPPA rule's general compliance date of 22 April 2026 has passed ([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/coppa-rule-amendment-compliance-deadline-approaches)), and the UK Children's Code applies to services likely to be accessed by children ([Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/ckgs2ty6aadwl0a43e3avb7r3/ico-s-age-appropriate-design-code-comes-into-force)). Google announced on 29 July 2026 that the Play Age Signals API reaches all developers worldwide by end of 2026, with ranges of 0-12, 13-15, 16-17 and 18+, and its data may not be used for advertising, analytics or profiling ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/29/google-is-rolling-out-its-age-assurance-tech-for-apps-worldwide-by-year-end/)). Use it as a second check where available. If teens are allowed later, match only within age bands with preset messages. For data, store an opaque ID, a generated nickname, a 15-minute slot, an IANA timezone, the push token, streak counters, blocks and reports, and nothing else. The retention periods in the notes (about 90 days for idle anonymous accounts, about 30 days for ping history) are my suggestions with no authority behind them, and the GDPR and ICO points are general knowledge rather than researched, so this is not legal advice.

## Push, realtime and voice cost almost nothing until live calls

Push is the lowest-risk item. Expo Push fronts FCM on Android behind one API ([Expo push overview](https://docs.expo.dev/push-notifications/overview/)). The setup is a development build (not Expo Go), `expo-notifications`, an FCM V1 service-account key uploaded to EAS, and `google-services.json` ([Expo FCM credentials](https://docs.expo.dev/push-notifications/fcm-credentials/)). Use the app-signing SHA-1 from Play Console, not the upload key, or no token is issued. Supabase's documented pattern is a Database Webhook on insert into a `notifications` table that calls an Edge Function, which posts to `https://exp.host/--/api/v2/push/send` ([Supabase push guide](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/examples/push-notifications)). Send "mate is up" with `priority: "high"`, a visible title and body, a custom `channelId` created at app start, and a short non-zero `ttl`; a stale "your mate woke" is worthless, so one to four hours is my suggestion. Normal priority "may be delayed on sleeping devices", a missing channel suppresses display, and tickets only confirm Expo accepted the message, so fetch receipts about 15 minutes later and drop tokens on `DeviceNotRegistered` ([Expo sending notifications](https://docs.expo.dev/push-notifications/sending-notifications/)). FCM may downgrade high-priority messages that do not produce visible notifications ([Firebase message priority](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/android/message-priority)). The Expo limits are 100 messages per request and 600 notifications per second per project, which only matters if many mates wake at the same minute. I found no per-message fee for Expo Push or FCM, but neither a Firebase pricing page nor an Expo push quota was fetched, so "free" is unverified.

Realtime replaces the current 5-second poll, with one rule: treat it as a hint and the database row as truth. Hold the socket only on the "waiting for mate" screen, and rely on push when backgrounded, because Doze suspends network access and Google tells apps to use FCM instead of persistent connections ([Android Doze](https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby)). On resume, do one REST read, then re-subscribe; this is my design recommendation, since the notes found no supabase-js reconnect documentation. For the MVP, a `postgres_changes` subscription filtered by pair ID is the smallest change. It authorises each event per subscriber and tops out around 3,000 to 4,000 messages per second with RLS, and Supabase recommends Broadcast beyond about 3,000 concurrent subscribers ([Supabase Postgres Changes](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/postgres-changes)). Broadcast from the database uses private channels with RLS on `realtime.messages` ([Supabase Broadcast](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/broadcast)). This interacts with the earlier patch: its narrow `match_view` and revoked table grants break table-level `postgres_changes`, so with the patch you need the Broadcast route or a poll limited to the wake window. The binding limit is concurrent connections: 200 on Free, 500 on Pro, and 10,000 on Pro without a spend cap or on Team ([Supabase Realtime limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/limits)). Overage is $2.50 per million messages and $10 per 1,000 peak connections ([Supabase Realtime pricing](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/pricing)). Morning wake times cluster, so peak concurrency, not MAU, is what hits the limit.

For voice and text the cheapest safe path is asynchronous. A 15-second "good morning" note recorded with `expo-audio` (about 240 KB at the HIGH_QUALITY preset, by my arithmetic) fits Supabase's 6 MB standard-upload guidance ([expo-audio](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/audio/); [Supabase uploads](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/storage/uploads/standard-uploads)). Store it in a private bucket with RLS limited to the pair, play it by signed URL, and delete it after 24 to 48 hours. It needs no WebRTC. Live voice is a different scale of work. LiveKit Cloud costs $0 for 5,000 participant-minutes, $50/month for 150,000 minutes then $0.0005 per minute ([LiveKit pricing](https://livekit.com/pricing)), and its Expo docs require a development build ([LiveKit Expo](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/sdk-platforms/expo/)). Daily charges $0.00099 per audio-only participant-minute after 10,000 free ([Daily pricing](https://www.daily.co/pricing/video-sdk/)). Agora starts at $0.59 per 1,000 minutes with 10,000 free ([Agora pricing](https://www.agora.io/en/pricing/)). Twilio Video starts at $0.004 per participant-minute ([Twilio pricing](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/video/pricing)). Stream Chat's paid tiers start at $399/month billed annually ([Stream pricing](https://getstream.io/chat/pricing/)), which only makes sense once moderation tooling is worth that price. Text on a Supabase messages table is nearly free but means building read state and moderation yourself.

| Scale (my assumptions) | Supabase base | Realtime | Voice-note egress | Rough total, push + realtime + notes | Optional live voice per month |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1k users | Free works, Pro $25 advised | peak about 150, inside 200 free | about 7 GB vs 5 GB free | $0 to $25 | free on LiveKit, Daily, Agora; Twilio about $8 |
| 10k users | Pro $25 | peak about 1,500: about $10 overage, spend cap off | about 72 GB, inside 250 GB | about $35 to $60 | LiveKit $50, Daily about $10, Agora about $6, Twilio about $80 |

These figures come from the vendors' pages as fetched on 8 November 2026 through a summarising tool, and the usage assumptions are guesses: peak concurrency of 15% of users, one note per user per day, 10% of users making one 10-minute call a month. The 100k row is a wide range because Team pricing and compute were not researched. The lesson is that peak concurrency, not total users, drives the bill, and the cost levers are connecting Realtime only on the waiting screen, using mono 64 kbps notes, auto-deleting notes, and batching pushes. Compatibility of Agora, Daily, Twilio and Stream with Expo development builds was not checked, and only LiveKit has a documented Expo path.

## Phased roadmap, ranked decisions and open questions

The roadmap below orders work by what removes the largest risk first. Efforts are my estimates for one developer, unsourced, and Phase 2 is the least certain because it depends on real-device testing.

| Phase | Scope | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Hygiene | Contrast and accessibility fixes, safe-area and error states from the earlier frontend audit; cancel-by-identifier instead of cancel-all; pin expo-notifications docs | 1-2 days |
| 1. Reliable alarm, minimum | `delivery: 'alarmClock'`; exact-alarm permission check; `ALARM`-usage channel with a long bundled tone; reliability checklist with a screen-off test alarm; foreground re-join and re-arm; Play declarations filed | 1-2 days plus review wait |
| 2. Backend hardening | Apply the draft `backend_design_patch_v2.sql` (single standing slot, 15-minute slots, leave and report re-pairing, `mark_awake` window, rate limit, purge) as migrations; client parses `match_view` and handles `PT429` and `28000`; wire CAPTCHA and Play Integrity | 3-5 days |
| 3. Safety and policy shell | 18+ age screen, in-app block and report, feedback contact, public child-safety page, CSAM contact, account deletion in app and on the web, Data safety form | 3-5 days |
| 4. Push and realtime | Firebase, FCM V1 key, dev build, token table, webhook plus Edge Function, receipts cleanup; foreground Realtime in place of polling | 4-7 days |
| 5. Native ringer | Kotlin Expo module: own `setAlarmClock`, receiver, foreground service looping `USAGE_ALARM` audio, full-screen alarm activity, boot and time-change receivers, telemetry; testing on Pixel, Samsung and Xiaomi | 1-2 weeks plus device testing |
| 6. Social depth | Preset messages, then 15-second voice note (3-5 days) and text (3-5 days) only after moderation exists; live voice (1-3 weeks) last, if at all | weeks |

The ranked decision list orders choices by reversibility and consequence.

| Rank | Decision | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Alarm delivery | `alarmClock` delivery now; native ringer next, not a third-party library |
| 2 | Exact-alarm permission | `USE_EXACT_ALARM` primary; drop `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` unless review objects |
| 3 | Audience | 18+ only with a neutral age screen, plus Age Signals where available |
| 4 | Interaction | Ping and presets first; avoid live voice with strangers |
| 5 | Matching | 15-minute slots, one region and niche, trio fallback, personal streak |
| 6 | Policy shell | Ship block, report, safety page, deletion path in the first public build |
| 7 | Push | Expo Push over FCM, high priority, short ttl, receipts |
| 8 | Realtime | Foreground-only; Broadcast with the patch's narrow view, `postgres_changes` otherwise |
| 9 | Voice | Async note before text before live; LiveKit if live is needed |
| 10 | Text vendor | Supabase table now; revisit Stream only at scale |

Several things remain unverified. No alarm behaviour was run on a device, and there are no success-rate benchmarks for `alarmClock` delivery versus a custom service. Play's review of the exact-alarm and full-screen-intent forms, including turnaround and whether declaring both permissions is penalised, was not found. Android 15 and 16 changes to alarms, full-screen intents, and foreground-service types were not fetched, and neither were per-vendor steps for Huawei, OnePlus, Oppo or Vivo. Whether Play classes a ping-only app as "random chat", and the Child Safety Standards deadline, are open, and the legal claims on GDPR, COPPA actual knowledge and US state age laws came from secondary sources. Backend fixes in the earlier patch ran on stock PostgreSQL 16 with a stubbed `auth` schema, not real Supabase. Push cost claims lack a fetched Firebase or Expo quota page, Supabase message-counting rules and Team pricing were not read, and remote push in Expo Go on SDK 58 is unconfirmed (plan on a development build). Compatibility of Agora, Daily and Twilio with Expo was not checked, and the usage and concurrency assumptions behind the cost table are guesses to replace with data from the first cohort.

## Conclusion

The research changes the priority order of the earlier audit. The earlier report treated the native ringer and Play review as a separate track, but the Play documents show they are one decision: the full-screen-intent and `USE_EXACT_ALARM` declarations are only honest if the app delivers a real alarm, so the ringer is the gate to the store, not a polish item. In the same way, safety work is cheap only because the interaction is minimal, and every step up the ladder (free text, then voice) multiplies moderation, policy and legal work faster than it adds retention.

The practical implication is that the first real unknown is not technical capacity but liquidity and OEM behaviour, and both can be measured cheaply. A closed test in one region, with alarm-fired telemetry and match-rate per slot, will say within weeks whether Phase 5 and the narrow-niche bet are needed, before money goes to realtime scale, live voice or a chat vendor.
